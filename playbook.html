Applied to Us What It Means & What We Can Do
01 / IDENTITY

Identity-Based Habits

Behavior change has three layers: Outcomes (what you get), Processes (what you do), and Identity (who you are). Outcome goals (“lift more”) lose to daily comfort. Identity sticks because every action—no matter how small—casts a vote proving who you are becoming.

Applied to Us

Anchor on Long-Term Identity, Not Perfect Workouts

Old Script
“We should try to work out more often.”
New Frame
“We’re people who invest in longevity and challenge our bodies consistently.”
  • Longevity is the core vote: Strength today is a bonus; the identity is about staying strong and capable for decades.
  • Small votes still count: A brief effort upstairs, an active walk pause, or a gentle joint-safe day all reinforce the same identity—breaking the “all-or-nothing” trap.
  • Shared norm, individual pace: Even when we move separately or adapt for different energy/joint needs, making strength a normal part of our home helps both of us.
02 / 1ST LAW — CUE

Make It Obvious: Environment & Habit Stacking

Habits don’t fire on memory or willpower—they fire on visible environmental cues and habit stacking: “After [current habit], I will [new habit].” When cues sit in plain sight and attach to transitions that already happen, you stop forgetting.

Applied to Us

Use Event Anchors (Not Clocks) & Keep Cues Upstairs

  • Put cues where we actually spend time: Gear hidden downstairs in the garage is easy to forget; keeping quiet tools visible upstairs puts reminders directly in our daily path.
  • Drop fixed clock schedules: Because dog walks, meals, and work blocks shift every day, anchor habits to reliable events instead:
WFH Desk Transitions

Standing up after calls, waiting on builds, or refilling water.

Evening Laptop Spots

Opening a laptop in the evening (our real default screen, not the old TV).

Hill Walk Pauses

Stop-and-sniff pauses on the hill + playful bursts when the dog runs.

Eating Together

Floating clock, guaranteed daily event—an easy shared trigger.

03 / 2ND LAW — CRAVING

Make It Attractive: Temptation Bundling & Space Appeal

The brain is wired to avoid dread and seek immediate appeal. Temptation bundling pairs an action you want to take with something you already enjoy—and upgrading the sensory feel of your space removes anticipation dread before you start.

Applied to Us

Pair With Screens & Remove Garage Discomfort

  • Bundle with laptops instead of fighting them: Pair light upstairs movement with watching, listening, or browsing on laptops—removing the competition between “relaxing” and “moving.”
  • Make the garage feel cozy, not bleak: Pre-warm the space, tidy and light one small dedicated corner, and keep the main garage door closed to block wind, cold drafts, loud door noise, and passersby.
  • Reframe the social dread of downstairs guests: Give ourselves explicit permission during daytime non-quiet hours and empty windows—the awkwardness lives mostly in our heads, not in reality.
04 / 3RD LAW — RESPONSE

Make It Easy: Reduce Friction & The Two-Minute Rule

Energy is precious, so humans naturally follow the Law of Least Effort. Every extra step between deciding and starting multiplies resistance. Use the Two-Minute Rule: shrink the starting requirement down to two minutes—standardize showing up before optimizing.

Applied to Us

Shrink the Starting Line & Bypass High-Friction Days

  • Use the Two-Minute Rule to beat inertia: Give ourselves full permission to stop after two minutes—almost all resistance happens before starting, not after.
  • Don’t force the garage on high-friction days: On busy WFH days, office days, or quiet guest hours, stay upstairs or use hill walks so the stairs and guest hallway never block momentum.
  • Remove decision friction next: Not knowing what matters (or what’s safe for sensitive joints) is pure friction—once we add simple labeling & tracking next, picking what to do becomes effortless.
05 / 4TH LAW — REWARD

Make It Satisfying: The Valley & "Never Miss Twice"

What is immediately rewarded is repeated. Because physical results compound slowly—creating an early Valley of Disappointment—you need immediate wins today. And when life interrupts, Never Miss Twice keeps one skipped day from turning into a new habit of quitting.

Applied to Us

Reward Today & Keep the Chain Resilient

  • Lean into immediate payoffs we actually feel: Post-session energy, accomplishment, mild good soreness, and visible progress markers bridge the early weeks before long-term changes show.
  • Never Miss Twice: Missing on an office day, low-energy day, or flare day is just normal life—casting one tiny vote the next day protects the streak without guilt.
  • Why tracking & labeling comes next: Turning progress (and joint responses) into simple, visible feedback makes every small session immediately rewarding and replaces guesswork with clarity.
06 / STAYING MOTIVATED

The Goldilocks Rule

Humans experience peak motivation when working on tasks right at the edge of their current ability—not too easy (boredom) and not too hard (dread and burnout). Sustainable progress lives in that manageable middle zone.

Applied to Us

Protect Energy & Respect Daily Capacity

Old Script
“If I push myself now, I’ll be drained for dog walks—or risk triggering a joint flare.”
New Frame
“Staying at today’s manageable edge builds strength while leaving energy intact and joints calm.”
  • Preserve energy for real life: Stopping well short of exhaustion creates energy for coding, dog walks, and play—rather than competing with them.
  • The edge moves day to day: When a joint is sensitive or energy is low, the Goldilocks edge simply shifts lighter—so we learn safely through curiosity instead of avoiding movement out of fear.
