01 / IDENTITY Atomic Habits

Start With Identity, Not Outcomes

Behavior change has three layers: Outcomes (what you get), Processes (what you do), and Identity (who you believe you are). Outcome goals (“lift more”) fade whenever comfort competes. Identity works in the moment—every small action casts a vote for the kind of person you are becoming.

Applied to Us

Reframe Around Longevity & Small Votes

Old Script
“We need to get motivated to go work out more often.”
New Frame
“We’re people who take care of our future bodies every day—in ways that fit our real energy and space.”
  • Longevity is the anchor: Strength today is a great bonus, but the identity is about staying resilient, mobile, and capable decades from now.
  • Every vote counts equally toward identity: A 90-second break upstairs, active floor-sitting with a laptop, or a gentle joint-respecting day casts a vote just as much as a garage session.
  • Separate goals, shared culture: We each have different bodies and energy patterns, yet moving regularly becomes simply “what we do” in our household.
02 / DISTRIBUTION Kelly Starrett

Movement as Daily Nutrition, Not a "Workout Box"

Kelly Starrett’s core thesis in Built to Move: you can’t isolate movement into a single 1-hour box and sit motionless for the other 23 hours. Spreading small bouts of loading, position changes, hanging, and walking throughout your normal day builds durability without needing marathon gym willpower.

Applied to Us

Flip the 80 / 20 Balance of Our Home

  • Kill the "didn't go to the garage = zero" guilt: Most daily physical nutrition can happen right upstairs during normal WFH life and outside on hill dog walks.
  • Treat the garage as a 20% bonus, not the bottleneck: When conditions are open (daytime, between guests), the garage is a great upgrade—never the single point of failure for the week.
  • Break up software-engineer sitting: Tiny movement snacks across coding blocks undo stiffness far better than sitting for 8 hours and hoping evening willpower survives.
03 / ENVIRONMENT & CUES Clear × Starrett

Make It Obvious: Design the Space Where You Actually Live

1st Law (Make It Obvious): People rarely forget habits that live in their direct line of sight. Starrett calls this building an active environment—putting tools, floor space, and subtle prompts in the rooms you already spend hours in, instead of hiding them behind closed doors.

“Your environment is the invisible hand that shapes human behavior. Don’t rely on remembering—make the cue unavoidable.”
Applied to Us

Bring Cues Upstairs & Pair With Real Screen Spots

  • Eliminate “out of sight, out of mind”: Keep a few quiet, grab-and-go tools upstairs where we actually live and work—alongside the pull-up bar and weights already upstairs—so cues trigger automatically.
  • Build around laptops, not the old TV: Since laptops are our real default screens, set up at least one comfortable floor/mat laptop spot (low table + cushion) so evening screen time passively invites floor-sitting and position changes.
  • Place cues along existing paths: Desk → kitchen, hallway doorways, and right beside the dog leash—places our eyes and hands already pass every day.
04 / HABIT STACKING Atomic Habits

Anchor to Existing Events, Not Clock Times

Habit stacking attaches a new cue directly onto an automatic transition you already do: “After [existing event], I will [tiny action].” When life doesn’t run on fixed clock hours, event anchors survive busy days far better than rigid calendar alarms.

Applied to Us

Our Four Reliable Non-Clock Anchors

1. WFH Desk Transitions

Standing up after a call, waiting on builds/tests, or refilling water—natural pause points every WFH day.

2. Evening Laptop Time

Pair watching/browsing with floor posture or brief micro-sets between videos so screens become a cue instead of an obstacle.

3. Hill Walk Sniff-Breaks

Reframe stop-and-sniff pauses on the hill from “waiting” into built-in movement/balance triggers—plus short run bursts when the dog wants to sprint.

4. Eating Together

Even though meal times shift daily, eating together is guaranteed—an effortless shared anchor for a 2–3 minute check-in or micro-habit.

On 2×/week office days: Drop evening garage expectations entirely—lean on commute/stair micro-habits and one tiny arrival cue when getting home.

05 / FRICTION & DREAD Atomic Habits

Make It Attractive & Easy: Strip Away Activation Energy

2nd & 3rd Laws: Humans default to the lowest-friction option. Every tiny hurdle—cold air, dust, awkward hallway noise, decision fatigue—multiplies resistance before you start. Use the Two-Minute Rule: scale the entry commitment down so starting takes almost zero willpower.

Applied to Us

Neutralize Each Specific Barrier Directly

  • Downstairs guest mental barrier: Explicitly give ourselves permission during daytime non-quiet hours and between bookings—and rely on upstairs during quiet hours so there’s zero awkwardness.
  • Garage cold, dust & door friction: Keep the main garage door closed (blocks loud door noise, wind, heat loss, and passersby), pre-warm beforehand, and sweep/light one small corner so it feels cozy rather than utilitarian.
  • Laptop inertia: Use the 2-Minute Rule—commit only to standing up and touching the first cue, with full permission to stop after two minutes.
  • Decision fatigue (“what matters?”): Solved cleanly in our next phase—once we define our tracking & labeling, the choice of what to do shrinks to a 3-second glance.
06 / EFFORT CALIBRATION Clear × Starrett

The Goldilocks Rule: Work at the Sustainable Edge

Humans stay most engaged right at the edge of current ability—challenging enough to feel worthwhile, not so brutal that it triggers dread or burnout. Too easy creates boredom; too hard destroys tomorrow’s consistency.

Applied to Us

Protect Energy for Dog Play & Avoid Over-Reaching

Old Script
“If I push hard right now, I’ll be too drained to walk or play with the dog later.”
New Frame
“Stopping with gas left in the tank creates energy—it leaves me fresher for walks, play, and coding.”
  • Aim for mild, satisfying soreness—not depletion: Longevity rewards decades of repeatable B+ days over sporadic all-out grinds.
  • Leave wanting slightly more: Ending a session feeling energized trains your brain to look forward to the next cue instead of avoiding it.
07 / BODY FEEDBACK Starrett × Clear

Scale Effort to Daily Capacity Instead of Quitting

Consistent movers don’t treat capacity as fixed—they scale smoothly between high-capacity, medium-capacity, and restorative days. Body signals aren’t a reason to abandon the habit; they’re feedback guiding which gear to shift into today.

Applied to Us

Replace Flare Anxiety With Calm Discovery

Old Script
“Not knowing what will irritate a joint makes it safer to skip altogether.”
New Frame
“Every joint signal is useful data. Scaling to the day’s tier keeps us safe while we learn what works.”
  • Non-binary participation: A gentle or restorative day still preserves identity and momentum—without pushing a sensitive joint.
  • Experimentation beats guessing: Rather than trying to predict flare triggers in our heads, we’ll pair calm movement with lightweight logging next so patterns become clear over time.
08 / REWARD & PATIENCE Atomic Habits

Make It Satisfying, Survive the Valley & "Never Miss Twice"

4th Law (Make It Satisfying): Real longevity gains compound slowly—creating an early Valley of Disappointment where effort doesn’t yet look dramatic. Immediate feedback bridges that valley, and the rule Never Miss Twice ensures a busy day never snowballs into quitting.

Applied to Us

Reward Today & Keep the Chain Resilient

  • Lean into immediate rewards we already feel: Post-effort energy, mild satisfying soreness, feeling accomplished after coding blocks, and enjoying shared movement or screen pairing.
  • Practice "Never Miss Twice": Missing on a busy office day, guest-heavy day, or flare day is completely normal. One tiny 2-minute cue the next day keeps the streak alive.
  • Why tracking & labeling comes next: Seeing concrete progress (and clear joint feedback) makes the early weeks immediately rewarding while long-term longevity compounds in the background.
